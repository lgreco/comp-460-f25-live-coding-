# Final Ungrading Reflection Instructions  

At the end of the semester, you will write a **final reflection** on your learning. This reflection replaces a traditional points-based final exam. It is your opportunity to evaluate your own progress with honesty, specificity, and evidence.


---

## 1. Purpose of the Reflection
The goal of this reflection is to help you:

- Synthesize what you learned across the entire course.  
- Acknowledge where you struggled—and how you responded to those struggles.  
- Make a case for the final grade you believe reflects your learning.

This is *not* about perfection or retroactively fixing work. It’s about demonstrating thoughtful engagement with the material and with your own learning process.

---

## 2. What to Include in Your Reflection

Your reflection should be **300-600 words** and should address the following components.  
Think of it as a well-structured essay rather than a bullet list.

### A. What you learned
Discuss the major concepts, techniques, and skills you gained during the course.

- Which ideas clicked for you?  
- What changed in how you think about programming / data structures / algorithms / scientific reasoning (as appropriate)?  
- Where did you see improvement over time?


### B. What the class discussions taught you

 
- Explain what you now understand that you did not understand earlier.  
- Describe at least one misconception you corrected during the semester.  

This is about growth, not self-criticism.

### C. How you responded to challenges
Learning is messy. Reflect on:

- Assignments or concepts that were difficult.  
- Strategies you used to overcome confusion (office hours, re-reading sections, rewriting code, discussing with peers, etc.).  
- Mistakes you made and how they led to deeper understanding.  
- Revisions you made that significantly improved your understanding.

Be concrete and candid.


### D. Your proposed course grade
Based on your learning story, propose the final grade you believe accurately reflects your achievement.

Your proposed grade should be supported by:

- Attendance and participation
- Engagement and self-motivation, e.g., pursuing topics on your own
- Your persistence and improvement  
- Your mastery of the course learning goals

You are not rewarded for modesty or punished for confidence; the goal is a grounded, honest argument. 

Your proposed grade should be A, B, or very rarely in grad courses C. The grade you propose should also reflect your confidence for succeeding in a technical interview for algorithms. 

Your final grade may be different from your proposed grade -- if the difference is more that a plus/minus step, I'll provide you with a justification. For example, if you propose a B and end up with a D, there will be an explanaton. If you propose an A and end up with an A-, there will be no justification.

---

## 3. Formatting and Submission
- **Length:** 200-400 words  
- **Style:** Clear, organized, reflective prose  
- **Submission method:** attach PDF file on Sakai. 
- **Due date:** Thursday, December 11, 3 PM

---

## 4. Evaluation Criteria
Your reflection will be evaluated on:

1. **Thoughtfulness and honesty** — Are you genuinely examining your learning?  
2. **Specificity** — Do you cite concrete examples from your class experience?  
3. **Depth of insight** — Do you explain *why* and *how* your understanding developed?  
4. **Connection to learning goals** — Do you demonstrate what you can now do?  
5. **Quality of the grade argument** — Is your proposed final grade well supported?

---

This reflection is your chance to step back from the work of the semester and tell the full story of your learning with clarity and pride.
